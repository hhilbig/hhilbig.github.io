CLARA

Communication of Local German Party Associations, Recorded Annually
A chapter-year panel dataset, 2015–2025 · Version 1.0.0 · Hanno Hilbig, University of California, Davis

Download data (Harvard Dataverse) | Data report (PDF) | Codebook

Local party organizations communicate extensively through their own websites, but these records are difficult to observe systematically. Chapters use different website formats, and content disappears as sites are updated or taken offline, leaving a thin and scattered historical record. CLARA reconstructs this record from archived websites. It covers 2,635 local chapters of the seven main German parties annually from 2015 to 2025, or 28,985 chapter-years. The unit is the Kreisverband, the county-level association maintained by each party.

How the data were built

Each chapter's website is rebuilt year by year from Internet Archive snapshots, about 1.03 million archived pages. A locally hosted open-weight language model then processes those pages and extracts structured records. No released value was generated using a commercial model. News pages are deduplicated to one record per article, 237,399 of them dated inside the panel window, each classified by policy topic, article function, and geographic focus. The data report describes the sampling frame, the discovery channels, and every extraction step.

What the data measure

  • Organizational presence: whether a board, officials, issue content, and a local office address are listed.
  • Board composition: size, gender shares inferred from first names, whether a chair is listed, and proxies for professionalization.
  • Issue emphasis: 25 topic families, mapped to the Comparative Agendas Project scheme, to facilitate comparison with other agenda-coded data.
  • Digital presence: the chapter's own accounts on six social platforms, with national party accounts excluded.
  • Mobilization infrastructure: whether the site offers a donation, membership, newsletter, program, events, or volunteer pathway.
  • News content: article counts and shares by topic, function, and geographic focus.

Coverage and limitations

Coverage is uneven, and it is not random. Of the 25,509 chapter-years in the recommended analysis scope, 17,989 are researcher-usable. Roughly a quarter have no archived snapshot from that year, and no further collection can recover them because the Internet Archive never captured the site. Every row records the sources underlying its coverage, allowing researchers to reconstruct the denominator or impose stricter coverage criteria. Where the archive contains a page of the relevant type, extraction almost always succeeds.

Two selection problems compound. A chapter had to still be online in early 2026 to enter the roster, and its site had to have been archived in a given year to be observed that year. Chapters that went offline before 2026 are missing entirely, which affects the early years most. A news count therefore reflects how often the Archive captured a site as much as how much the chapter posted. The data are better suited to comparisons across chapters and multi-year averages; year-to-year change within a chapter is noisier than the panel structure suggests. The codebook is specific about which columns to condition on.

Validation

A human coder checked extracted records against their source pages across five stratified samples. In a sample drawn from the released records, 45 of 48 were confirmed. A second open-weight model, from a different model family than the extractor, independently evaluated a fresh sample of released records against their source pages. Both checks are reported in full in the deposit.

The data also show face validity. Election-campaigning classifications track the electoral calendar even though year was not provided to the classifier.

Policy-topic classifications show similar temporal patterns. Attention to migration, climate, health, and energy peaks during or near periods in which each issue was especially salient in German national debate.

Migration also shows the expected cross-party pattern: in every year of the panel, the AfD devotes a larger share of its news topics to migration than any other party.

Data and documentation

The deposit contains the chapter-year panel and its enriched version, the chapter roster, long-format topic and news files, a per-row provenance file, and crosswalks to the Comparative Agendas scheme and to Bundestag electoral districts. It also includes the codebook, the data report, coverage diagnostics, validation summaries, and minimal R and Python load scripts. The core panel is mirrored here as chapter_year_panel.csv (13 MB), but the Harvard Dataverse deposit is the citable version.

Person-level records and reproduced page text are not part of the release: the records are personal data under the GDPR, and the parties wrote the text, which may be under copyright. A controlled-access version of this material is in preparation. Data and documentation are CC-BY-4.0, and the pipeline code is available from the author on request.

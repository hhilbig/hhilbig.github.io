CLARA

Communication of Local German Party Associations, Recorded Annually
A chapter-year panel dataset, 2015–2025 · Version 1.0.0 · Hanno Hilbig, University of California, Davis

Download data (Harvard Dataverse) | Data report (PDF) | Codebook

What do local party organizations say, and what can we actually observe about them? Chapters publish on their own websites, in their own formats, and delete what they publish, so the record is thin and scattered. CLARA reconstructs it. It covers 2,635 local chapters of the seven main German parties, annually from 2015 to 2025, or 28,985 chapter-years. The unit is the Kreisverband, the county-level association each party runs.

How the data were built

Each chapter's website is rebuilt year by year from Internet Archive snapshots, about 1.03 million archived pages. A locally hosted open-weight language model then reads those pages and extracts structured records. No value in the release passes through a commercial model. News pages are deduplicated to one record per article, 237,399 of them dated inside the panel window, each classified by policy topic, article function, and geographic focus. The data report describes the sampling frame, the discovery channels, and every extraction step.

What the data measure

  • Organizational presence: whether a board, officials, issue content, and a local office address are listed.
  • Board composition: size, gender shares inferred from first names, whether a chair is listed, and proxies for professionalization.
  • Issue emphasis: 25 topic families, mapped to the Comparative Agendas Project scheme so the measures line up with other agenda-coded data.
  • Digital presence: the chapter's own accounts on six social platforms, with national party accounts excluded.
  • Mobilization infrastructure: whether the site offers a donation, membership, newsletter, program, events, or volunteer pathway.
  • News content: article counts and shares by topic, function, and geographic focus.

What to watch out for

Coverage is uneven, and it is not random. Of the 25,509 chapter-years in the recommended analysis scope, 17,989 are researcher-usable. Roughly a quarter have no archived snapshot from that year at all, and no further collection can recover them: the Internet Archive never captured the site. Every row records what its coverage rests on, so a denominator can be rebuilt or made stricter. Where the archive does hold a page of the right type, extraction almost always succeeds.

Two selection problems compound. A chapter had to still be online in early 2026 to enter the roster, and its site had to have been archived in a given year to be observed that year. Chapters that went offline before 2026 are missing entirely, which affects the early years most. A news count therefore reflects how often the Archive captured a site as much as how much the chapter posted. Comparisons across chapters and multi-year averages are the safest uses; year-to-year change within a chapter is noisier than the panel structure suggests. The codebook is specific about which columns to condition on.

Does it measure what it claims to?

A human coder checked extracted records against the pages they came from, across five stratified samples; on a sample drawn from the released records, 45 of 48 were confirmed. A second open-weight model, from a different family than the extractor, independently judged a fresh sample of released records against their source pages. Both checks are reported in full in the deposit.

A third kind of evidence is face validity: the labels move with events the classifier was never told about. It never sees the year, yet the share of articles it labels election campaigning tracks the electoral calendar.

Policy topics behave the same way. Attention to migration, climate, health, and energy each peaks inside or at the edge of the period in which that issue dominated German national debate.

And migration separates the parties in the expected direction: the AfD devotes a larger share of its news topics to it than any other party, in every year of the panel.

Data and documentation

The deposit holds the chapter-year panel and its enriched version, the chapter roster, long-format topic and news files, a per-row provenance file, and crosswalks to the Comparative Agendas scheme and to Bundestag electoral districts. It also carries the codebook, the data report, coverage diagnostics, validation summaries, and minimal R and Python load scripts. The core panel is mirrored here as chapter_year_panel.csv (13 MB), but the Harvard Dataverse deposit is the citable version.

Person-level records and reproduced page text are not part of the release: the records are personal data under the GDPR, and the parties wrote the text, which may be under copyright. A controlled-access version of that material is in preparation and is not ready yet. Data and documentation are CC-BY-4.0, and the pipeline code is available from the author on request.
